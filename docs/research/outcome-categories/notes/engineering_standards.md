## KQ1 — Process design rules: minimum wall / minimum feature, holes, overhangs, bridging and orientation by process (FDM 0.4 mm nozzle, SLA resin, MJF/SLS PA12, full-colour binder jetting)

### Takeaway
Across sources the practical floors are: FDM (0.4 mm nozzle) 0.8 mm minimum / 1.2–1.5 mm recommended wall, holes modelled ~0.2–0.25 mm oversize; SLA 0.2 mm is the warp/detach threshold (so ~1 mm for handled goods); SLS PA12 0.8 mm, MJF PA12 ~0.5 mm with ≥0.76 mm gaps; full-colour sandstone ~3 mm walls and hollow. Slicer tooling treats ~45° as the default overhang limit, and no source gave an authoritative maximum bridge span — estimate that by slicing rather than by rule.

### Cited Findings
- FDM: "One extrusion on FDM is around 0.4 mm on a standard 0.4 mm nozzle. However, the practical minimum for a part you intend to use is two walls, roughly 0.8 mm"; absolute minimum 0.8 mm (two perimeters) for non-structural features and 1.2 mm (three perimeters) for structural walls (search summary) — [Qidi min-wall guide](https://qidi3d.com/blogs/news/minimum-wall-thickness-3d-printing); [Layer X FDM design rules](https://layerx3d.in/blog/fdm-design-rules-wall-thickness-overhangs-bridging-tolerances); [Formlabs minimum wall thickness](https://formlabs.com/blog/minimum-wall-thickness-3d-printing/)
- Xometry recommends FDM walls at least 0.75–1.00 mm or three times the nozzle diameter, and 0.047–0.06 in (1.2–1.5 mm) for supporting walls (search summary) — [Xometry Pro FDM design tips](https://xometry.pro/en/articles/fdm-design-tips/)
- FDM holes "almost always come out slightly undersize, because the circle is approximated by straight segments and the extrusion is pressed inward. Model the hole about 0.2 mm larger, or set X-Y hole compensation in the slicer" (search summary) — [Qidi: turn models into keychains](https://qidi3d.com/blogs/guides/add-hole-to-3d-model-keychain)
- "3D-printed holes always come out undersized — on some printers, every hole measured about 0.25 mm smaller than designed" (search summary) — [InsertGuide M3 hole size](https://insertguide.com/m3-heat-set-insert-hole-size-for-3d-printed-parts/)
- Resin (SLA/MSLA) offers 0.035–0.05 mm XY resolution versus a 0.4 mm nozzle on FDM, so it holds tight fits (keycap stems) more consistently (search summary) — [42 Studio keycap guide](https://www.42prints.com/blog/3d-printed-keycaps-guide)
- SLA (Form 4 / Form 3): "A supported wall (connected to other walls on two or more sides) with a thickness of 0.2 mm or less may warp due to peel forces"; an unsupported wall "of 0.2 mm or less may warp or detach from the model during printing"; guidelines developed with Grey Resin at 50 µm on Form 4 (search summary) — [Formlabs Form 4 Design Guide](https://formlabs.com/white-papers/form-4-design-guide/); [Formlabs Form 3 design specifications](https://support.formlabs.com/s/article/Design-specifications-for-3D-models-form-3?language=en_US)
- "SLA 3D printers can create the thinnest walls of all 3D printing technologies", and the Form 3+ flexible resin tank "significantly reduces peel forces" (search summary) — [Formlabs minimum wall thickness](https://formlabs.com/blog/minimum-wall-thickness-3d-printing/)
- SLS PA12 minimum wall ~0.8 mm; MJF minimum allowable wall 0.020 in (0.508 mm); minimum channel/gap 0.030 in (0.762 mm) for both SLS and MJF to prevent features fusing (search summary) — [Protolabs nylon (SLS/MJF) design guide](https://www.protolabs.com/resources/design-tips/how-to-design-for-nylon-3d-printing/)
- Walls below the minimum "may not survive printing and cleaning processes"; Shapeways recommends reinforcing walls or adding supports because minimum guidelines "may not always be adequate for all designs" (search summary) — [Shapeways MJF PA12](https://www.shapeways.com/materials/multi-jet-fusion-pa12)
- Full-colour sandstone (binder jetting): recommended wall ~3 mm, models should be hollow for consistent colour and lower cost; "fragile dainty models cannot be made with this process"; use hard colour edges, avoid gradients; brown and flesh tones are hard (CMYK mixing); parts are sealed with cyanoacrylate after printing (search summary) — [Shapeways: Designing for Full Color Sandstone](https://support.shapeways.com/hc/en-us/articles/360008363353-Designing-for-Full-Color-Sandstone)
- Overhang threshold: OrcaSlicer's "Make overhang printable" modifies geometry so overhangs steeper than a max angle are filled; "a value between 45° and 60° works well for most printers and models" — [OrcaSlicer wiki: overhangs](https://github.com/SoftFever/OrcaSlicer/wiki/quality_settings_overhangs)
- Support generation semantics: "Support will be generated for overhangs whose slope angle is below the threshold" (user-set threshold angle; no default given on the page) — [OrcaSlicer wiki: support](https://github.com/SoftFever/OrcaSlicer/wiki/support_settings_support)
- Bridging: "Thick bridges" use a line height equal to the nozzle diameter, which "improves the strength and reliability of bridges, allowing you to span longer distances"; 100% bridge density "may not be sufficient" due to sagging; no maximum bridge length is specified — [OrcaSlicer wiki: bridging](https://github.com/SoftFever/OrcaSlicer/wiki/quality_settings_bridging)
- Blender's official 3D-Print Toolbox ships default check thresholds of thickness_min = 0.001 m (1 mm), angle_overhang = 45°, angle_sharp = 160°, angle_distort = 45°, threshold_zero = 0.0001 — [blender-addons object_print3d_utils/__init__.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender-addons/main/object_print3d_utils/__init__.py)
- Orientation examples: 3D-printed Choc keycaps are published with a "recommended printing orientation that ensures minimal support (only for stem)" and a stem "that doesn't break inside a switch" at ~0.1 mm layers (search summary) — [MakerWorld Kailh Choc keycaps](https://makerworld.com/en/models/154057-kailh-choc-keycaps); print-in-place AirTag cases print at 0.1 mm layers, "a 0.2 mm layer height could prevent hinged mechanisms from functioning" (search summary) — [Thingiverse AirTag keyring case](https://www.thingiverse.com/thing:4837318)

### Inferences
- Proposed validator thresholds for the app (derived from the cited floors, with margin for AI-mesh roughness): FDM-0.4 — reject/repair walls < 0.8 mm, warn < 1.2 mm, target ≥ 1.5 mm for anything load-bearing (hooks, stands, cutters); holes +0.2 to +0.25 mm; unsupported overhang > 45° triggers reorientation or support cost; SLA — reject < 0.4 mm (2× the 0.2 mm warp threshold), target ≥ 1.0 mm for handled goods; MJF/SLS — reject < 0.6 mm (MJF) / 0.8 mm (SLS), gaps between separate features ≥ 0.8 mm; sandstone — reject < 3 mm and require hollowing.
- Because no source gives a maximum bridge length, the pipeline should not hard-code one; slice the fused model (KQ5) and read support volume/time instead.
- Fine surface detail is a process choice, not a mesh choice: for small figurines/jewellery/keycaps prefer resin (0.035–0.05 mm XY) and reserve FDM for flat carriers (plaques, keychains, coasters, frames).

### Gaps
- Exact Formlabs SLA numbers for embossed/engraved detail, minimum hole, clearance, vertical wire and drain-hole diameter could not be read (domain blocked); only the 0.2 mm wall thresholds surfaced.
- Hubs/Materialise/Sculpteo SLS/MJF tables (min detail, escape-hole size, clearance for moving parts) were blocked; only the Protolabs numbers surfaced.
- No sourced maximum unsupported bridge length or minimum pin/column diameter for FDM.
- Bambu Lab wiki and Prusa Knowledge Base design pages were blocked; PrusaSlicer default overhang threshold not captured.

## KQ2 — Per-category hardware specs, size envelopes and spec-sheet skeletons

### Takeaway
Hardware that constrains geometry has well-documented numbers for keychains (≈4 mm hole for a split ring), keycaps (MX cross ≈4.0–4.1 × 1.17–1.25 mm with 0.3–0.35 mm slop; Choc 1.2 × 3 mm posts at 5.7 mm; Topre 6.2/5.7 mm), magnets (pocket +0.2–0.4 mm), pin backs (~11 mm clutch, ~1 mm pin, 8 mm post), lithophanes (0.4–0.8 mm to 3–3.5 mm), lamp collars (E27 40 mm / E14 28–29 mm), heat-set M3 (4.0–4.2 mm hole), AirTag (31.9 × 8 mm) and cookie cutters (0.8–1.2 mm wall, 10–20 mm tall). Most "desk gadget", jewellery-finding, coaster, bookmark, pet-tag and cake-topper dimensions had no authoritative source and are proposed values.

### Cited Findings
- Keychain / bag charm: "the hole should be modeled at about 4 mm in diameter with at least 2 mm of solid material around it"; a 1.2 mm-wire split ring needs 2.4 mm as the absolute floor, "but at that size the ring binds and the hole is hard to thread, so 4 mm is the practical minimum"; model FDM holes ~0.2 mm larger or use XY hole compensation (search summary) — [Qidi: turn models into keychains](https://qidi3d.com/blogs/guides/add-hole-to-3d-model-keychain). 25 mm split rings are a commodity accessory (Bambu Lab sells 10-packs; Etsy category "split rings for keychains 25mm") — [Bambu Lab Split Ring Keychain](https://us.store.bambulab.com/products/split-ring-keychain); [Etsy 25 mm split rings](https://www.etsy.com/market/split_rings_for_keychains_25mm). - Keycap (Cherry MX): the MX stem is a cross "with a width of 4.1 mm and thickness of 1.17 mm according to official Cherry MX drawings" (search summary) — [42 Studio](https://www.42prints.com/blog/3d-printed-keycaps-guide); [dev.to keycap guide](https://dev.to/mysticcoder/how-to-design-and-print-your-own-3d-keycaps-a-beginners-guide-2pel). KeyV2's cross definition is `cherry_cross(slop, extra_vertical=0) = [[4.03 + slop, 1.25 + slop/3], [1.15 + slop/3, 4.23 + extra_vertical + slop/3 + SMALLEST_POSSIBLE]]`; `outer_cherry_stem(slop) = [7.2 - slop*2, 5.5 - slop*2]`; `outer_box_cherry_stem(slop) = [6 - slop, 6 - slop]`; stabilizer stem `[4.85 - slop*2, 6.05 - slop*2]` — [KeyV2 src/functions.scad](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsheldiii/KeyV2/master/src/functions.scad). KeyV2 settings: `$stem_slop = 0.35`, `$stem_inner_slop = 0.2`, `$rounded_cherry_stem_d = 5.5`, `$wall_thickness = 3` (commented as 1.5 mm walls), `$stem_throw = 4`, `$stem_support_type = "tines"`, `$stem_support_height = .8`, `$cherry_bevel = true`, `$bottom_key_width = 18.16`, `$top_tilt = -6`, `$minkowski_radius = .33`, stem types `[cherry, alps, rounded_cherry, box_cherry, filled, disable]` — [KeyV2 src/settings.scad](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsheldiii/KeyV2/master/src/settings.scad). The README states the default slop is 0.3 mm and "even if you have a resin printer, you should probably keep the default value; keys printed with 0 slop will barely fit on the stem"; loosen with `cherry(0.5) key()`; profiles SA, DSA, DCS, G20, Hi-Pro, OEM — [KeyV2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsheldiii/KeyV2/master/README.md) (conflict: README 0.3 vs settings.scad 0.35). FDM tolerance practice: add ~0.1 mm; FDM accuracy is only ~0.05 mm; one designer pushes the stem's inside walls out by 0.4 mm; print 3–4 variants at ±0.05 mm and test-fit; the "box_cherry" stem type prints more reliably on FDM than the round type (search summary) — [Thingiverse MX tolerance test](https://www.thingiverse.com/thing:4397634); [FacFox keycap tips](https://facfox.com/news/tips-for-3d-printing-custom-keycaps-for-mechical-keyboards/); [Printables GEM-profile MX set](https://printables.com/model/399607-complete-cherry-mx-stem-keycap-set-optimized-for-3).

### Inferences — spec-sheet skeleton per category
Legend: C = cited above; P = proposed value (no authoritative source found; validate with test prints). Shape tolerance: any = user mesh may be arbitrary; constrained = mesh must fit an envelope/base rule; strict = functional geometry generated parametrically, user mesh only decorates.

- Keychain / bag charm — Size envelope: 30–60 mm max dimension, 3–6 mm thick (P). Hardware: 25 mm split ring (C, ring wire ~1.2 mm), 4 mm hole with ≥2 mm rim (C). Process/material: FDM PETG/ASA (heat, see KQ3) or resin tough resin; MJF PA12 for premium (P). Min wall: 1.2 mm FDM (C/KQ1); loop rim ≥2 mm (C). Base/orientation: flat back plate, print face-up (P). Mesh ops: scale-to-envelope, plane-cut/flatten back, boolean-union backing plate + loop boss, boolean-subtract 4.2 mm hole (FDM oversize, C), thin-feature thicken, floater removal (P). Cost/time drivers: grams (tiny), colour changes if multi-colour, hardware assembly. Safety: none beyond small-parts; ASA/PETG for car keys (KQ3). Shape tolerance: any (backing plate absorbs shape).
- Keycap (MX / Choc / Topre) — Size: 1u cap footprint 18.16 mm (C, KeyV2 `$bottom_key_width`), heights per profile (C, KeyV2 profiles). Hardware: MX cross 4.03–4.1 × 1.15–1.25 mm with 0.3–0.35 mm slop (C); Choc posts 1.2 × 3 mm at 5.7 mm separation (C); Topre 6.2 / 5.7 mm stem (C, low-confidence). Process: resin strongly preferred (0.035–0.05 mm XY, C); FDM needs +0.1–0.4 mm stem adjustment and test variants (C). Min wall: 1.5 mm cap walls (C, KeyV2 `$wall_thickness=3` total). Base/orientation: cap printed upside-down or with stem-only supports (C for Choc). Mesh ops: parametric stem+cap from KeyV2-like generator (strict), user mesh scaled to ≤~14 × 14 × 6 mm relief on the top face and boolean-united (P). Cost/time: resin post-processing (wash/cure), stem QA/test-fit. Safety: none. Shape tolerance: strict.
- Desk gadgets (phone stand, pen holder, cable clip, headphone stand, card holder, paperweight, organizer) — Size: 60–150 mm (P). Hardware: optional M3 heat-set inserts, 4.0–4.2 mm holes, ~1 mm extra depth (C). Process: FDM PETG (rigidity/heat) or PLA for indoor (P). Min wall: 1.5–2 mm for load-bearing arms (C/KQ1 range). Base/orientation: flat base mandatory; phone stand and headphone stand need stiff cantilevers, avoid unsupported >45° (C). Mesh ops: parametric functional body (stand/holder), user mesh as decorative applique or as a hollowed "shell" fused to the functional core; hollow + drain for paperweight fill (P). Cost/time: grams and print hours dominate (largest category by volume). Safety: tip-over stability check (centre of mass inside footprint) (P). ## KQ3 — Material/process fit and safety (food contact, skin contact, heat, kids, colour and post-processing)

### Takeaway
PLA (Tg ≈ 60 °C) fails in a parked car (cabin up to ~70 °C) and near bulbs (~50 °C limit cited); use PETG/ASA/ABS for keychains, lamps and outdoor pots. No SLA resin is food-safe without extra steps, and cookie cutters are tolerable only as semi-disposable, smooth or sealed items; resin wearables and plated resin jewellery carry skin-sensitisation and nickel/cadmium concerns, so metal casting or PA12 are safer choices.

### Cited Findings
- Glass transition temperatures cited: PLA 60 °C, PETG 90 °C, ASA 105 °C (search summary) — [ALL3DP: Tg of PLA/PETG/ABS](https://all3dp.com/2/pla-petg-glass-transition-temperature-3d-printing/); [JLC3DP: PETG temperature resistance](https://jlc3dp.com/blog/petg-temperature-resistance)
- "In warm regions, the cabin temperature of a car parked in the direct sunlight with cooling off can be as high as 70 °C, which can easily warp, deform or sag PLA prints" (search summary) — [3D Printerly: will PLA/PETG/ABS melt in a car](https://3dprinterly.com/will-pla-petg-or-abs-melt-in-a-car-or-the-sun/)
- PETG "may soften or deform when temperatures reach 50–60 °C or higher, especially under load or direct sunlight. For prolonged exposure, consider ASA" (search summary) — [JLC3DP PETG](https://jlc3dp.com/blog/petg-temperature-resistance)
- ASA "combines mechanical strength, UV resistance and water resistance... making it ideal for... outdoor applications and the automotive industry" — [Wikipedia: ASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrylonitrile_styrene_acrylate); for load-bearing vehicle parts choose ABS (~105 °C) or ASA (~100 °C) (search summary) — [All About The Build: filament for car parts](https://www.allaboutthebuild.com/blog/2025/2/what-filament-is-good-for-car-parts)
- Lamps: PETG recommended for heat, "PLA's border line is around 50 Celsius", use only 3–7 W LED bulbs, incandescent bulbs "pose a fire hazard" (search summary) — [Printables ribbed E27 lamp](https://www.printables.com/model/1258908-modern-ribbed-table-lamp-easy-3d-print-e27-socket); [MakerWorld lampshade E27/E14](https://makerworld.com/en/models/959116-lampshade-e27-e14)
- Food: Prusa's guidance is that cookie cutters "should not present much problem to 3D-print, but layer-lines will collect food and bacteria will grow, so they should be thrown away after some use" (search summary) — [Prusa KB: food safe FDM printing](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/food-safe-fdm-printing_112313)
- Formlabs: "No Formlabs resins are food safe unless users take additional steps"; for FDM print at the lowest feasible layer height; long-term food safety needs a smooth surface, e.g. chemical smoothing with acetone, d-limonene or ethyl acetate (search summary) — [Formlabs food-safe guide](https://formlabs.com/blog/guide-to-food-safe-3d-printing/)
- "Cookie cutters are among the safest food-contact objects you can make, because they don't stay in contact with the food like a drinking cup or a dinner plate" (search summary) — [Qidi: is PETG food safe (cookie cutters, CFR)](https://qidi3d.com/blogs/print-lab/is-petg-food-safe-cookie-cutters)
- Standard brass nozzles are "not considered food-safe, as it wears out — it is a consumable"; some filaments contain additives, dyes or plasticizers that can leach; "No resin used in SLA printing is food-safe" (search summary) — [AmeraLabs: 3D printed cookie cutters](https://ameralabs.com/blog/3d-printed-cookie-cutters/); [StackSheriff: cookie cutters](https://stacksheriff.com/3d-printing/3d-printed-cookie-cutters/); [StackSheriff: is PETG food safe](https://stacksheriff.com/3d-printing/petg-food-safe/)
- Skin/resin: remove rings/watches when handling uncured UV resin because trapped resin increases "the risk of irritation or allergic reactions" (search summary) — [RadTech: safe handling of 3D printing resins](https://radtech.org/safe-handling-of-3d-printing-resins/); [Raise3D: resin toxicity](https://www.raise3d.com/blog/3d-printing-toxicity/)
- Jewellery: a 2025 Bureau of Indian Standards consumer report reportedly found 37% of metal-coated 3D-printed jewellery contained trace nickel or cadmium; if plating on a resin base cracks "you're exposing your skin to the raw material underneath"; resin "becomes brittle over time, especially in sunlight" (search summary; jeweller blog, low authority) — [RH Jewellers: is 3D printed jewelry safe](https://rhjewellers.in/is-it-safe-to-wear-3d-printed-jewelry-what-you-need-to-know-before-wearing-it)
- Planters: PETG is hydrophobic and suited to watertight prints; outdoor pots should be PETG or ASA (search summary) — [FacFox planters](https://facfox.com/docs/kb/3d-printed-plant-pots-and-self-watering-planters-waterproof-3d-printing); [3DWithUs planters](https://3dwithus.com/3d-printed-plant-pots-self-watering-planters-waterproof-3d-printing)
- Colour: full-colour sandstone needs hard colour edges and struggles with browns/flesh tones; parts are CA-sealed (search summary) — [Shapeways sandstone design](https://support.shapeways.com/hc/en-us/articles/360008363353-Designing-for-Full-Color-Sandstone); Hero Forge sells "Color Plastic miniatures that are table-ready" made by material jetting (search summary) — [Hero Forge products](https://heroforge.com/content/product-information/about-products/); PVB filament can be IPA-smoothed for keycap surfaces (search summary) — [Cults3D Topre set](https://cults3d.com/en/3d-model/various/topre-keycaps-leopold-fc660c-bbaik)

### Inferences
- Material matrix for the app: PLA only for indoor décor (frames, bookmarks, nameplates, lithophanes, ornaments); PETG default for keychains, coasters, planters, desk gadgets; ASA for anything living in a car, outdoors or near heat; TPU/flexible resin only for stamps and protective holders; tough resin for keycaps, small figurines, pendants; MJF/SLS PA12 for durable/thin lattice items (pet tags, jewellery, hooks); sandstone/PolyJet only for figurines/busts ≥ 3 mm walls.
- Food-contact policy: label cookie cutters and cake toppers as "limited/indirect food contact, hand-wash, replace periodically"; print at ≤ 0.15 mm layers in a filament sold as food-contact-compliant; do not offer resin for these; for cake toppers keep the decorative body above the cake on plain picks so only the picks touch food.
- Skin-contact policy: for pendants/earrings/rings prefer PA12 or cast metal; if resin is offered, require fully post-cured parts and a clear-coat, and avoid nickel-plated finishes.
- Kids' items: enforce no detachable magnets/pins and no features < 2 mm; treat as a separate compliance track (no source obtained, see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No primary regulatory text (FDA 21 CFR, EU 10/2011, EN 71/CPSIA) was retrievable; toy small-parts rules for 3D-printed items unsourced.
- The PETG Tg figure (90 °C) is at the high end of commonly quoted datasheet values; verify against the chosen filament's datasheet.

## KQ5 — Automated mesh operations to fuse a user mesh with a carrier: libraries, slicers, licences, prior art and recommended toolchain

### Takeaway
A permissively licensed core is available: trimesh (MIT) for I/O and analysis, manifold3d (Apache-2.0) for guaranteed-manifold booleans/hull/SDF, build123d or CadQuery (Apache-2.0) for parametric carriers, Open3D (MIT) for manifold checks/decimation; robust repair of broken AI meshes needs GPL/commercial tools (MeshFix/pymeshfix GPL-3 or commercial, PyMeshLab GPL, Blender/bpy GPL-3 voxel remesh, MeshLib commercial); every mainstream slicer with a CLI (PrusaSlicer, Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer, CuraEngine) is AGPL-3, so run it as an isolated, unmodified subprocess for time/material estimates.

### Cited Findings
- Manifold: Apache-2.0; bindings for Python (`manifold3d`), JS/TS (WASM), C plus community Java/C#/Rust/Julia/etc.; "guaranteed-manifold mesh Boolean algorithm", convex hull, Minkowski, SDF level-set, decomposition, simplification, vertex properties; "you'll get an error status if the imported mesh isn't manifold"; a `Merge` function fixes slightly non-manifold input, otherwise external repair is recommended; TBB parallelism; users include OpenSCAD, Blender, Godot, trimesh — [elalish/manifold](https://github.com/elalish/manifold); PyPI manifold3d 3.5.4, Apache, recommends 3MF and glTF `EXT_mesh_manifold`, provides a trimesh interop Colab — [PyPI manifold3d](https://pypi.org/project/manifold3d/)
- trimesh: MIT; watertight/convex checks; "repair simple problems with triangle winding, normals, and quad/triangle holes"; split by face connectivity; booleans "using Manifold3D or Blender"; cross sections and "slice a mesh for a 3D remainder mesh, i.e. slicing for 3D-printing"; convex hull; voxelize watertight meshes; nearest point / signed distance; NumPy-only hard dependency — [mikedh/trimesh](https://github.com/mikedh/trimesh); boolean.py: Manifold preferred, Blender fallback, `check_volume=True` raises unless "all meshes are watertight positive volumes" — [trimesh/boolean.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mikedh/trimesh/main/trimesh/boolean.py); repair.py: `fix_winding`, `fix_inversion` (negative-volume flip, watertight only), `fix_normals`, `fill_holes` ("using fans, which may result in bad answers if the holes are non convex"), `broken_faces`, `stitch` — [trimesh/repair.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mikedh/trimesh/main/trimesh/repair.py); version coupling risk: "trimesh.boolean breaks after upgrading to manifold3d==2.3.1" — [trimesh issue #2112](https://github.com/mikedh/trimesh/issues/2112); latest release listed 5.1.0 — [trimesh releases](https://github.com/mikedh/trimesh/releases)
- Open3D: MIT — [Open3D LICENSE](https://github.com/isl-org/Open3D/blob/main/LICENSE); 0.20.0 (Sep 2026), Python 3.10–3.14 — [PyPI open3d](https://pypi.org/project/open3d/); mesh API: `is_edge_manifold`, `is_vertex_manifold`, `is_self_intersecting`, `is_watertight` ("edge manifold, vertex manifold, and non-self-intersecting"), `is_orientable`, smoothing filters, `simplify_vertex_clustering`, `simplify_quadric_decimation`, midpoint/Loop subdivision, `cluster_connected_triangles` (returns cluster index, count, area — useful for floater detection), `remove_triangles_by_mask`, `compute_convex_hull`, point sampling — [Open3D mesh tutorial notebook](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/isl-org/Open3D/main/docs/jupyter/geometry/mesh.ipynb)

- libigl: MPL-2.0 primary with GPL-3 parts (external-library integrations) — [libigl/libigl](https://github.com/libigl/libigl); Python bindings `pip install libigl` 2.6.3, MPL-2.0, nanobind, Python ≥ 3.8 — [PyPI libigl](https://pypi.org/project/libigl/); [libigl-python-bindings](https://github.com/libigl/libigl-python-bindings)

### Inferences — recommended automation toolchain (with licence notes)
1. Ingest and normalise (trimesh, MIT): load GLB/OBJ/STL from the generator, drop textures/materials, merge duplicate vertices, `split()` into bodies, compute volumes; decimate to ≤ ~500k triangles (Open3D `simplify_quadric_decimation`, MIT) so downstream booleans and the slicer's 1M-triangle/300 s caps are respected.
2. Repair to a watertight solid: first try cheap fixes (trimesh `fix_normals`, `fill_holes` for small convex holes; Manifold `Merge`); if `is_watertight` still fails, fall back to a volumetric rebuild — either SDF level-set through manifold3d (Apache) or Blender voxel remesh via `bpy` (GPL-3, fine to run server-side as a separate process since GPL obligations trigger on distribution, not network use — confirm with counsel), or MeshFix/pymeshfix (GPL-3 or paid commercial), or MeshLib (paid). Remove floaters by dropping components below a volume threshold (Open3D `cluster_connected_triangles` or trimesh `split`).
3. Fit to carrier: scale to the category envelope (KQ2), orient (principal axes / `--orient` later), plane-cut the base (trimesh `slice_plane`) or project the silhouette (cookie cutters, stamps), and optionally hollow (MeshLib offset or Manifold SDF offset) with drain holes for resin/sandstone.
4. Generate the carrier parametrically: build123d/CadQuery (Apache-2.0) for plates, loops, bosses, frames, collars, pockets and MX/Choc stems (port KeyV2's numbers rather than depend on its unverified licence and on OpenSCAD GPL-2), export STL/3MF.
5. Fuse: manifold3d booleans via trimesh (`engine="manifold"`, inputs must be watertight positive volumes); pin `manifold3d` and `trimesh` versions together (issue #2112).
6. Validate: wall/feature thickness by per-face ray casting (Blender toolbox approach; trimesh `proximity`/ray tests), overhang faces > 45°, small-part/floater check, bounding box vs envelope, watertight/manifold (Open3D checks), loop-rim ≥ 2 mm, stem cross fits.
7. Estimate: slice with OrcaSlicer or Bambu Studio CLI (`--orient`, `--arrange 1`, `--slice 0`, `--export-slicedata`/`--export-3mf`) per candidate printer/material profile to read print time, filament mass and support volume; run the AGPL slicer unmodified in its own container/process and do not link it into the app (the AGPL network clause binds the slicer's operator to publish the slicer's source, which is already public, but modifying it would require publishing your modifications).
8. Category-specific generators: lithophane heightmap→mesh (own implementation modelled on lithofun, MIT); relief from image via depth estimation (as MakerLab does); keycap generator from KeyV2 parameters; cookie cutter from silhouette offsets.

## KQ6 — Failure modes that make AI-generated meshes unprintable, standard fixes, and which categories tolerate "any shape"

### Takeaway
AI meshes typically fail on non-manifold edges, open boundaries, degenerate faces, inverted normals, floaters, sub-0.8 mm walls and internal shells — exactly the defects Meshy/Tripo now auto-repair — and the fix is a watertight rebuild plus thin-feature thickening and floater removal before any boolean; categories with a flat backing plate (keychain, magnet, pin, bookmark, nameplate, ornament, pet tag, relief plaque) tolerate any shape, while keycaps, phone stands, cookie cutters, holders, stamps and lithophanes must be generated parametrically with the user mesh as decoration.

### Cited Findings
- "A model that looks stunning in Unity can be completely unprintable — non-manifold geometry, walls thinner than 0.8 mm, or floating internal structures that no slicer can interpret. AI-generated models produce these errors more often than CAD models because neural-network geometry optimizes for visual appearance, not topological correctness" (search summary; vendor blog) — [Meshy: fix non-manifold edges](https://www.meshy.ai/blog/fix-non-manifold-edges-stl-repair)
- Meshy's printability check targets "non-manifold edges, degenerate faces, holes and open boundaries" and Auto Repair covers "broken meshes, non-manifold edges, floaters, and inverted normals" so exports are watertight (search summary) — [Meshy 3D printing docs](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/guides/use-cases/3d-printing); [Meshy auto-repair guide](https://www.meshy.ai/tutorials/auto-repair-3d-models)
- Manifold refuses non-manifold input ("error status if the imported mesh isn't manifold"); `Merge` only fixes slight defects — [elalish/manifold](https://github.com/elalish/manifold); trimesh booleans require "watertight positive volumes" — [trimesh/boolean.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mikedh/trimesh/main/trimesh/boolean.py); trimesh `fill_holes` fans "may result in bad answers if the holes are non convex" and `fix_inversion` only works on watertight bodies — [trimesh/repair.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mikedh/trimesh/main/trimesh/repair.py)
- MeshFix rebuilds a single watertight mesh but "may fail on tessellated CAD models" — [MeshFix-V2.1](https://github.com/MarcoAttene/MeshFix-V2.1)
- Open3D defines watertight as edge-manifold, vertex-manifold and non-self-intersecting, and provides connected-component clustering with per-cluster area — [Open3D mesh notebook](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/isl-org/Open3D/main/docs/jupyter/geometry/mesh.ipynb)
- Blender's 3D-Print Toolbox checks non-manifold, intersections, degenerate, distorted, thickness (default 1 mm), sharp edges and overhang (default 45°), and its "Make Manifold" uses iterative hole filling and manifold-vertex removal; thin-surface repair is "not yet implemented" — [operators.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender-addons/main/object_print3d_utils/operators.py); [__init__.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blender/blender-addons/main/object_print3d_utils/__init__.py)
- Triangle-count limits in production tools: Hero Forge STLs "up to 100,000 triangles" (search summary) — [Hero Forge STL](https://heroforge.com/content/product-information/about-products/stl/); OrcaSlicer CLI caps 1,000,000 triangles and 300 s per plate by default — [wiki cli_actions](https://github.com/SoftFever/OrcaSlicer/wiki/cli_actions)
- Process floors that thin AI features violate: FDM 0.8 mm practical minimum (search summary) — [Qidi min wall](https://qidi3d.com/blogs/news/minimum-wall-thickness-3d-printing); SLA 0.2 mm warp/detach threshold (search summary) — [Formlabs Form 4 guide](https://formlabs.com/white-papers/form-4-design-guide/); MJF 0.508 mm / SLS 0.8 mm (search summary) — [Protolabs](https://www.protolabs.com/resources/design-tips/how-to-design-for-nylon-3d-printing/); sandstone 3 mm (search summary) — [Shapeways](https://support.shapeways.com/hc/en-us/articles/360008363353-Designing-for-Full-Color-Sandstone)
- Image-driven carriers need simplified inputs: MakerLab keychains want high-contrast images with "no thin lines, shadows or super fine details" (search summary) — [MakerLab tutorial](https://www.busymommasnook.com/post/how-to-make-a-3d-printed-keychain-from-an-image-using-makerworld-s-makerlab); lithophane sources should be smoothed and scaled to 1000–1500 px — [lithofun](https://github.com/yomboprime/lithofun)
- Unit/scale errors are common enough that Orca ships `--convert-unit` to detect non-millimetre models and `--ensure-on-bed` to lift sunken objects — [wiki cli_transform](https://github.com/SoftFever/OrcaSlicer/wiki/cli_transform)

### Inferences
- Failure → fix map: non-manifold/open boundaries → volumetric rebuild (SDF/voxel remesh) rather than fan hole-filling; inverted normals → `fix_normals` after watertightness; floating shells → drop components under a volume threshold, or bridge them to the main body with a generated strut; thin limbs/spikes → dilate via SDF offset or thicken below the process floor, else reject with a message; no flat base → plane-cut at a chosen height and union a plinth/plate; tiny details → decimate/smooth and accept loss; internal voids → voxel remesh eliminates them (or hollow intentionally with drain holes); huge triangle counts → quadric decimation to ≤ 500k before booleans and slicing; wrong units → detect bounding box (< 5 mm or > 500 mm) and rescale.
- Shape tolerance classes: any — keychain, fridge magnet, pin/badge, bookmark, nameplate/plaque, hanging ornament, pet tag, cake topper, figurine-on-plinth, raw print; constrained — pendant/earring (must have a solid attachment region), photo-frame ornament, coaster (flat top), planter and lamp shade (shell-able, open ends), paperweight (flat cut, mass limit); strict — keycap, phone/headphone stand, pen holder, cable clip, card holder, AirTag/controller holder, cookie cutter, stamp, lithophane.
- Product rule: for "any" categories, always add the backing plate/plinth and let the user mesh be a relief or a mounted volume; for "strict" categories, never boolean the user mesh into functional surfaces (stem cross, cavity, cutting wall, stand contact faces).

- Blender manual pages on Boolean solvers (Exact/Fast/Manifold) and voxel remesh guarantees were blocked, so the voxel-remesh recommendation rests on the add-on/source evidence rather than the manual.
